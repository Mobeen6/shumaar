Why the level swings

Reserves rise when official lenders disburse and fall when debt falls due. In the week to 19 June 2026, SBP reserves dropped $1.3bn to $15.9bn on external debt repayments; by 9 October they were back at $21.5bn. Much of the stock is borrowed, including deposits and loans from China, Saudi Arabia and the UAE that are rolled over each year, so the gross figure overstates what the State Bank owns outright.

Monthly, against 37 trading partners. A rising REER means the rupee is getting dearer in real terms, so exports lose competitiveness. The solid line is the REER's own long-run average: the shaded area above it marks an overvalued rupee, below it an undervalued one.

Rupees per US dollar, monthly average

How to read the REER: is the rupee over- or undervalued?

The 2010 base year is arbitrary, so the test is the gap to the REER's long-run average rather than to 100. The IMF uses fuller models (EBA) that also allow for productivity and the external balance; a gap of a few per cent is within their margin of error. The nominal rate (NEER) has fallen by about 62% since 2010, yet the real rate (REER) is back above 100, because Pakistan's inflation ran far ahead of its partners'. Devaluations bought competitiveness only briefly: the real rupee climbed to 124 before the 2018 crisis, and has risen again since its 2023 low while exports fell in FY26.

Savings

A country can only invest what it saves or borrows from abroad. Pakistan saves about 14-15% of its income; Bangladesh and India save about 35%. Low savings is why each investment push ends in an external deficit.

Savings and investment, Pakistan, % of GDP

When investment runs above savings, the gap is filled by foreign borrowing: the current account deficit.

National savings, % of GDP

Gross national savings. In 1995 Pakistan saved 17%, Bangladesh 22% and India 27%; now the gap is about 20 points.

Why Pakistan saves so little

Reasons commonly cited: a young, fast-growing population with many dependants; government deficits, which are negative public saving; few households with bank accounts or pensions, so savings go into land, gold and livestock; and inflation and a falling rupee that erode cash. Remittances count toward national savings, so domestic savings alone are lower still.

    What the research finds
    • Pakistani couples go on having children until they have a son, then a second son, and they want at least one daughter. The pursuit of sons runs mainly through extra births rather than sex-selective abortion. Zaidi and Morgan (2016), Population and Development Review.
    • The link between the sex of existing children and stopping or contraception grew stronger from 1990-91 to 2012-13; modern contraceptive use among mothers would have been about 19% higher without son preference. Channon (2017), International Perspectives on Sexual and Reproductive Health.
    • As families get smaller, the desire for sons concentrates on fewer births, so falling fertility can make sex ratios more male-biased; schooling lowers the son bias at any family size. Jayachandran (2017), American Economic Journal: Applied Economics (India).
    • When husbands had to be involved, women used family planning less and had more births: excess fertility partly reflects husbands wanting more children than wives. Ashraf, Field and Lee (2014), American Economic Review (Zambia).
    • Women's role in household decisions is linked to lower demand for children and more contraception in Pakistan, but explains only part of it once schooling and income are accounted for. Mahmood (2002), Pakistan Development Review; see also Hakim, Salway and Mumtaz (2003), Asia-Pacific Population Journal.

    Returns to education

    Gary Becker treated schooling as an investment (Becker, 1964): a family pays fees and gives up a child's earnings now in exchange for higher earnings later. If the return beats what the money could earn elsewhere, schooling pays. Jacob Mincer turned this into a wage equation, where the coefficient on years of schooling is the return to one more year (Mincer, 1974).

    Estimated return to one more year of school, Pakistan

    % higher wages per extra year of schooling, Mincer wage equations (OLS). Studies use LFS, PSLM or household panels; women's estimates come from the minority who earn wages.

    Why the return is high but schooling still lags

    Becker's model predicts under-investment when families cannot borrow against a child's future wages, when schools are far or poor, or when the payoff goes to someone else. For daughters all three apply: only 24% of women are in the labour force, and benefits of a daughter's schooling often accrue to her marital household.

    Is it worth it? A Becker investment calculator

    Set the schooling step, the return and the costs. The calculator compares the present value of higher earnings with fees and earnings given up while studying.

    Simplified: wages are flat over the career, the extra schooling raises them by (1+r)^years, and earnings given up equal the lower wage. Default wage: LFS 2024-25 average for employees (Rs 39,042). In-work chances use LFS 2024-25 participation and unemployment (men 69.8% × 94.1%; women 24.4% × 90.3%). The return estimates are averages: they include ability and family background, which push them up, and measurement error in schooling, which pushes them down.

        Nutrition

        Four in ten children under five are stunted, and only one in 28 toddlers eats an adequate diet. Pakistan carries a double burden: undernutrition in children alongside rising overweight and obesity in women. Most figures are from the National Nutrition Survey 2018, which measured and blood-tested over 100,000 households.

        Child malnutrition over time, %

        Children under 5. National Nutrition Surveys 2001, 2011 and 2018.

        Stunting barely moved in 17 years, while wasting rose and child overweight nearly doubled between 2011 and 2018.

        How babies are fed: the drop-off

        % of children meeting each WHO feeding standard. NNS 2018.

        Nearly nine in ten babies are breastfed, but fewer than half start in the first hour or are exclusively breastfed for six months. After six months, only 14% of toddlers eat a diverse enough diet and 3.6% meet the minimum acceptable diet.

        Hidden hunger: micronutrients

        Deficiencies found in blood tests, % affected

        Children 6-59 months and women 15-49. NNS 2018 biochemical tests.

        Four in five women are vitamin D deficient. Half of children lack vitamin A and iron. Pregnant women are more likely to be iron and zinc deficient (47% and 38%), yet only a third took iron-folic acid in their last pregnancy.

        Women and adolescents

        Women's weight: the double burden

        Women 15-49 by body mass index, %. NNS 2018.

        38% of women are overweight or obese, up from 28% in 2011, while 14.5% are underweight. In cities, obesity (17%) is more common than underweight (12%).

        Boys and girls

        % affected. NNS 2018. Orange: girls. Green: boys.

        Under five, boys fare slightly worse on almost every measure, as in most countries. The gap widens in adolescence: 21% of boys are underweight against 12% of girls. 55% of adolescent girls are anaemic.
